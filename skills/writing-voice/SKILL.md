# Writing voice

> Draft skill. Review it before relying on it.

This is the base voice for anything written as the user. The goal is always a draft they can approve as-is: it should read like they wrote it on a good day, not like an assistant wrote it for them.

Channel skills build on this one. `email-voice` and `slack-voice` load it first, then add their own registers, phrases, and examples. When a channel skill and this skill disagree, the channel skill wins for that channel. Use this skill by itself only for text with no channel skill, such as notes, short docs, or comments.

## Core rules

1. **Lead with the point.** The ask, the answer, or the artifact goes in the first line or two. No warm-up, no restating the thread, no apology preamble before a simple ask.
2. **Plain words.** Say it the way you'd say it out loud to a colleague. Contractions stay in (`I'm`, `we're`, `didn't`). No jargon or legalese, even in a formal note.
3. **Short.** If it can be one sentence, don't write three. Short paragraphs and line breaks beat one dense block. Use a list only for real requirements, steps, or edits, not to make a short note look organized.
4. **Warm, not performative.** Real thanks and real excitement are welcome, briefly. Gushing, stacked exclamation marks, and enthusiasm for routine things are not. Exclamation marks are for when they'd actually be excited or grateful.
5. **Unmistakable yes or no.** Say yes, then the plan. Say no plainly, with a reason if it helps, and leave a door open when there is one. A decline still sounds like a person.
6. **Dry humor, at their own expense, when it fits.** A light self-roast or an honest "I don't know how that works" is in character. Then back to the point. No jokes in formal, legal, or tense notes, and never at the recipient's expense.
7. **Honest over polished.** When they don't know something, the draft says so. Push back in the first person, naming the problem and the fix, without hiding behind "the company."
8. **Match formality to the audience.** People they already work closely with get the loosest register. New contacts, vendors, and formal or legal threads get complete, properly capitalized sentences, still in plain English. The channel skills spell out each register.
9. **Links pasted directly.** Put the link right in the sentence or on its own line, with at most a short setup. Never "please see the link below."
10. **Don't fake the voice.** Don't add slang, typos, swearing, or catchphrases to sound like them. Don't upgrade their casual writing into formal English either. Use a signature phrase only when it fits the room and the mood.
11. **Concrete next steps.** If there's a date, a time, or an owner, say it plainly. Offer alternatives instead of demanding a slot.

## Phrases they don't use

Never write these, in any channel:

- "Hope this finds you well" / "Hope you're doing well"
- "Just circling back" / "Per my last email" / "Per our conversation"
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out" / "Let me know if you have any questions" as a closer
- "Please find attached" / "Please see the link below"
- "I wanted to take a moment to" / "Going forward" / "As per" / "Synergy"
- "Thanks in advance" / "Best regards" / "Kind regards" / "Warmly"
- "Looping you in" / "Flagging this"

Ordinary uses of "reach out" and "let me know" are fine in a sentence; it's the stock closer that's out.

## Checklist

Before showing a draft:

1. Is the point in the first line or two?
2. Could any sentence be cut without losing meaning? Cut it.
3. Is the yes or no obvious?
4. Is the register right for this person, and for the channel (load the channel skill)?
5. Any phrase from the list above? Remove it.
6. Any slang, typo, or joke added just to sound like them? Remove it.
7. Are links pasted directly, and are dates, times, and owners concrete?
8. Anything you couldn't confirm (a date, a number, a commitment)? Leave a visible placeholder rather than inventing it.

## Before and after

**Before:**

> I wanted to take a moment to follow up on the proposal. We're very excited about the opportunity! Unfortunately, at this time, we won't be able to move forward with the timeline as proposed. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions.

**After:**

> Thanks for sending this over. The timeline won't work for us this quarter, but I'd love to revisit next quarter if you're open to it.

**Before:**

> Please see the link below for the updated document at your earliest convenience.

**After:**

> updated doc is here: [link]
